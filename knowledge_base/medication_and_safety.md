# Medication Timing and Safety

## Consistency of dosing

Medication works on a schedule. Twice-daily medication means roughly every 12 hours, and
once-daily means the same time each day. Anchoring a dose to a fixed daily event such as the
morning meal dramatically improves adherence, because the owner already has a reliable cue.

If a dose is missed, do not double the next dose. Give the missed dose if it is remembered
soon, otherwise skip it and resume the normal schedule, and check with your vet.

## With food or without

Some medications must be given with food to avoid stomach upset, and some must be given on an
empty stomach to absorb properly. This changes where the dose belongs in the day, so it is
worth recording on the task itself. When it is unclear, ask the prescribing vet.

## Do not schedule medication conflicts

Avoid scheduling two different medications at the exact same minute unless a vet has confirmed
they can be given together, since some drugs interact or interfere with absorption. Separating
doses by at least an hour is a safer default when the interaction is unknown.

## Common household dangers

Chocolate, xylitol sweetener, grapes, raisins, onions, and garlic are toxic to dogs. Lilies are
severely toxic to cats, and even pollen contact can cause kidney failure. Human painkillers
including paracetamol and ibuprofen are toxic to both dogs and cats and must never be given.

Never give a pet human medication that a vet has not specifically prescribed.

## Emergency signs

Seek immediate veterinary help for: difficulty breathing, collapse, repeated vomiting, a
distended hard abdomen, inability to urinate, seizures, or suspected poisoning. A male cat
straining in the litter tray without producing urine is a life-threatening emergency.

## Scope of this knowledge base

These are general care routines and scheduling heuristics only. They are not veterinary advice
and cannot account for an individual animal's medical history. Any medical decision, dosage,
or symptom must be taken to a qualified veterinarian.
