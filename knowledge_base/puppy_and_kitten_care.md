# Puppy and Kitten Care

## Puppy toileting frequency

Puppies cannot hold their bladder for long. A common guideline is one hour of bladder control
per month of age, plus one. A three-month-old puppy therefore needs a toilet break roughly
every four hours, and a four-month-old roughly every five hours. No puppy under six months
should routinely be left more than four to five hours without a break.

Take a puppy out immediately after waking, after eating, and after play. These three moments
account for most indoor accidents.

## Puppy feeding

Puppies under six months are usually fed three to four times per day. From six months, most
transition to twice daily. Frequent small meals suit their fast metabolism and small stomachs.
A three-meal pattern of 07:00, 12:30, and 18:00 is common.

## Puppy exercise limits

Over-exercising a growing puppy can damage developing joints and growth plates. A widely used
rule of thumb is five minutes of formal walking per month of age, up to twice a day. A
four-month-old puppy therefore gets about 20 minutes of walking twice daily. Free play in a
garden at the puppy's own pace is separate and is not restricted by this rule.

Growth plates close at roughly 12 to 18 months depending on breed size. Avoid repetitive
high-impact activity such as long runs on hard surfaces or jumping until then.

## Puppy training and socialisation

The critical socialisation window runs to about 16 weeks. Short, positive exposures to new
people, surfaces, sounds, and handling during this period have outsized lifelong effect. Keep
training sessions to 5 minutes at a time, several times per day; puppy attention spans are
very short.

## Kitten feeding

Kittens under six months need three to four meals per day, dropping to two or three by twelve
months. Kittens need calorie-dense kitten-formula food, not adult food, because their growth
demands are high.

## Kitten play

Kittens play in frequent short bursts. Four or five 5 to 10 minute sessions spread through the
day suits them better than one long session. Redirect biting onto toys rather than hands from
the very beginning, because a habit of biting hands is much harder to undo later.

## Vaccination timing

Puppies and kittens typically receive a primary vaccination course starting at 6 to 8 weeks
with boosters every 2 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks. Confirm the exact schedule with your
vet, since it varies by region and vaccine.
