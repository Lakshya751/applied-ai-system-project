# Senior Pet Care

## When a pet counts as senior

Dogs are generally considered senior from about 7 years, though giant breeds age faster and
may be senior by 5 to 6 years, while small breeds may not be senior until 9 or 10. Cats are
typically considered senior from about 11 years.

## Senior dog exercise

Senior dogs still need daily exercise, but shorter and more frequent is better than long and
strenuous. Three 15 to 20 minute gentle walks suit an arthritic dog better than one 60 minute
walk. Warm up gradually and avoid slippery floors and steep stairs.

Stiffness is usually worst first thing in the morning and after long rest. A gentle short walk
mid-morning, rather than immediately on waking, is often more comfortable.

## Senior cat routines

Older cats sleep more and play less, but should not stop playing entirely. Short, gentle play
sessions of about 5 minutes preserve muscle tone and mental sharpness. Provide steps or ramps
to favourite high perches, because jumping becomes painful with arthritis.

Senior cats commonly develop kidney disease and hyperthyroidism. Increased thirst, increased
urination, and weight loss despite a good appetite are all reasons to see a vet.

## Monitoring and vet visits

Senior pets benefit from vet check-ups every 6 months rather than annually, because conditions
progress faster and early detection matters. Weigh a senior pet monthly and record it; gradual
weight change is easy to miss by eye and is often the first measurable sign of illness.

## Medication routines

Senior pets are more likely to be on long-term daily medication for arthritis, thyroid, or
kidney conditions. Give long-term medication at the same time every day. Pairing the dose with
an existing fixed routine such as breakfast makes it far less likely to be forgotten.

## Comfort adjustments

Provide soft, supportive bedding away from draughts, and keep food, water, and litter trays on
the same floor of the house so a stiff pet does not have to climb stairs to reach them.
